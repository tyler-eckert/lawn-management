What is ET₀?

ET₀ is the depth of water, in inches per day, that would evaporate and transpire from a standardized reference surface, a short, uniform, well-watered grass, under the day's actual weather. Multiply ET₀ by a crop coefficient (Kc) specific to the grass type and the calendar month, and you get ETc: the actual water need for that grass, that day. The formula here is FAO-56 Penman-Monteith, the internationally standard method for computing ET₀.

Source

Allen, R.G., Pereira, L.S., Raes, D., Smith, M. (1998). Crop Evapotranspiration, Guidelines for Computing Crop Water Requirements, FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56. - fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e00.htm

The Penman-Monteith equation

FAO-56 Eq. 6: daily reference ET₀ in mm/day for clipped grass
ET₀ = 0.408 Δ (Rn − G) + γ 900T + 273 u2 (es − ea) Δ + γ (1 + 0.34 u2)
SymbolMeaningUnits
ΔSlope of the saturation vapor-pressure curve at mean air temperaturekPa/°C
RnNet radiation at the grass surface (incoming sunlight minus reflection and outgoing heat)MJ/m²/day
GSoil heat flux, 0 for a daily time stepMJ/m²/day
γPsychrometric constant, set by air pressure (elevation)kPa/°C
TMean daily air temperature at 2 m°C
u2Wind speed at 2 mm/s
es − eaVapor pressure deficit: how much drier the air is than saturatedkPa
0.408Converts MJ/m² of energy into mm of evaporated watern/a

The left half of the top line is the energy-driven part (sunlight). The right half is the wind-and-dry-air part. Then: ET₀ × Kc = ETc (water needed that day, per zone). Each day's ETc is averaged across a grass type's zones, and seven days are added up to get inches per week.
